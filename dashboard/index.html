Justification of the Defensible Structure (k = 4)

  • Clinical Discrimination: While k=2 mathematically maximizes silhouette, it merely divides Africa from other regions. k=4 isolates extreme maternal-infant mortality from chronic childhood stunting.
  • Elbow Deceleration: Marginal reduction in WCSS drops below 10% after k=4 (-9.73%), establishing an authentic point of diminishing returns.
  • Distance Sensitivity: Manhattan L1 geometry yields an ARI of 0.3345 with Euclidean K-Means, proving that L1 medoid estimation resists extreme mortality skewness.
  • Cluster Stability: 50 iterations of 85% bootstrap subsampling confirmed high cluster persistence with a mean ARI of 0.5970 ± 0.1630.

Limitations, Sampling Bias & Responsible Use Guidelines

⏱️

Temporal Survey Asynchrony

DHS national surveys run on 5-year cycles. Survey dates range from 2011 to 2023. Recent political shocks, economic crises, or health investments post-survey are unobserved in cross-sectional views.

🔍

Conflict & Geographic Exclusions

Two-stage cluster sampling relies on national census frames. Enumeration areas in active conflict zones (e.g. northern Nigeria, eastern DRC) are dropped for surveyor safety, introducing an optimistic survivorship bias.

📝

Maternal Recall Bias

Retrospective 5-year birth histories rely on maternal memory. Neonatal deaths occurring within 48 hours of birth are frequently underreported due to grief and stigma.

⚖️

Non-Punitive Aid Allocation

Unsupervised cluster tiers must never be used punitively to penalize lower-tier nations or enforce conditional aid benchmarks. Groupings represent structural health system deficits requiring global solidarity.
