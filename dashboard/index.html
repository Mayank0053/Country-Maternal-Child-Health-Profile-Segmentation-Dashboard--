Key Methodological Insights

  • Defensible Structure (k=4): Provides the clinically critical separation between countries facing chronic high under-5 mortality vs. nations with high stunting but moderate clinical infrastructure.
  • Euclidean vs. Manhattan Geometry: Manhattan (L1) medoid clustering exhibits an Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 0.71 at k=2 and 0.33 at k=4, proving that L1 metric provides increased resistance to extreme mortality outliers (e.g. fragile states).
  • Cluster Stability: Bootstrap resampling (85% subsample, 50 iterations) confirmed high cluster stability with a mean ARI of 0.60 ± 0.16.

Limitations, Sampling Bias & Responsible Use Framework

⏱️

Temporal Variance & Survey Asynchrony

DHS surveys are conducted at 5-year cycles. Analyzed countries have survey dates ranging from 2011 to 2023. Rapid health policy shifts, recent epidemics (e.g. COVID-19), or political disruptions after survey publication are not captured in historical records.

🔍

Survey Sampling & Geographic Exclusions

DHS utilizes two-stage cluster sampling based on national census frames. Remote nomadic populations, active conflict zones (e.g. parts of Northern Nigeria, eastern DRC), and internally displaced camps are often excluded due to surveyor security risks, inducing optimistic survivorship bias.

📝

Recall & Reporting Bias

Maternal and child mortality rates rely on 5-year retrospective birth histories recalled by mothers. Infant deaths that occurred shortly after birth may suffer from underreporting or misclassification as stillbirths, especially in traditional rural contexts.

⚖️

Ethical Aid Allocation & Non-Stigmatization

Unsupervised cluster labels must never be used punitively to restrict international development assistance or enforce conditional funding benchmarks. Groupings represent structural system challenges requiring resource mobilization, not governmental culpability.
